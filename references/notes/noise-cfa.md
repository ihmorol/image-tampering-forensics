## popescu2005cfa

**Full citation:** Alin C. Popescu and Hany Farid, "Exposing Digital Forgeries in Color Filter Array Interpolated Images", 2005. Venue string and DOI are not printed on the retrieved preprint pages - `not verified from retrieved text`.
**Retrieval:** Local PDF `references/papers/popescu2005_cfa.pdf` (20 pp), extraction clean (pypdf, 50417 chars, all 20 pages; math flattens but is readable).

**What the method computes:**

1. *Model.* Section III: assume each interpolated pixel is a weighted sum of its neighbours within a single colour channel. Inter-channel correlations are explicitly ignored - "we ignore these inter-channel correlations and treat each color channel independently" (Section III). Section III also gives the exact bilinear identities for the red channel of a Bayer array (Eq. 44-46): `R(2x+1,2y) = R(2x+1,2y-1)/2 + R(2x+1,2y+1)/2` (horizontal), `R(2x,2y+1)` from the two vertical neighbours, `R(2x,2y)` = mean of the four diagonal neighbours.
2. *Two-component mixture.* Section III-A. Each sample of a channel `f(x,y)` belongs to M1 (linearly correlated with its neighbours, Eq. 47: `f(x,y) = sum_{u,v=-N..N} alpha_{u,v} f(x+u,y+v) + n(x,y)`, with `alpha_{0,0}=0` and `n` iid zero-mean Gaussian of unknown variance sigma^2) or to M2, an outlier process modelled by a **uniform** density equal to the inverse of the range of `f` (Section III-A).
3. *E-step.* Eq. 48, Bayes rule with **equal priors 1/2** for the two models. The M1 likelihood is Eq. 49, a Gaussian in the prediction residual. Appendix A gives the runnable form: residual `r(x,y) = |f(x,y) - sum alpha^{(n)}_{u,v} f(x+u,y+v)|`; conditional `P(x,y) = 1/(sigma_n sqrt(2 pi)) exp[-r^2(x,y)/(2 sigma_n^2)]`; posterior `w(x,y) = P(x,y) / (P(x,y) + p0)`.
4. *M-step.* Weighted least squares, Eq. 50, weights `w(x,y)` = the E-step posteriors. Differentiating (Eq. 51-54) gives the normal equations `sum_{u,v} alpha_{u,v} ( sum_{x,y} w(x,y) f(x+s,y+t) f(x+u,y+v) ) = sum_{x,y} w(x,y) f(x+s,y+t) f(x,y)` - one equation per coefficient `alpha_{s,t}`, solved as a linear system. Variance update (Appendix A): `sigma_{n+1} = ( sum_{x,y} w(x,y) r^2(x,y) / sum_{x,y} w(x,y) )^{1/2}`.
5. *Initialisation and stopping.* Appendix A: `alpha^{(0)}` chosen randomly; `N` and `sigma_0` chosen by hand; `p0` = 1 over the size of the range of `f`. Iterate until `sum_{u,v} |alpha^{(n)}_{u,v} - alpha^{(n-1)}_{u,v}| < epsilon`.
6. *Parameters actually used.* Section IV: "The parameters of the EM algorithm were: N = 1, sigma_0 = 0.0075, and p0 = 1/256." N=1 is a 3x3 neighbourhood, so 8 free coefficients per channel and 24 across three channels (Section IV).
7. *Decision statistic.* Section IV-B. Build a **synthetic** binary map per channel from the known CFA lattice (Eq. 55: `s_g(x,y) = 0` where `S(x,y)` is a green sample, 1 otherwise; analogous for red and blue). Fourier transform both the estimated posterior map `p_g` and the synthetic map `s_g`, then score `M(p_g,s_g) = sum_{wx,wy} |P_g(wx,wy)| * |S_g(wx,wy)|` (Eq. 56, phase-insensitive). Threshold per channel, set empirically for 0% false positives. An image or window is called CFA-interpolated if **at least one** of the three channels scores above threshold, tampered if all three fall below (Section IV-B).
8. *Localisation.* Sections IV-A and IV-C: the posterior map `p(x,y)` is computed for the whole image, then windows are cut out of the *map* and Fourier transformed - localisation comes from presence or absence of the periodic peaks inside a window, not from a per-pixel score. Window sizes used: 256x256 and 512x512 with 50% overlap (Section IV-C). Footnote 4: probability maps were up-sampled x2 before the FFT, which shifts the corner peaks into mid-frequencies (a display convenience, but it also explains where the peaks live).

Steps I could not see: the exact epsilon, and whether the linear system is regularised - `not verified from retrieved text`.

**Assumptions and preconditions:**
- Needs the periodic CFA correlation still present, i.e. near-camera-native output. Sections IV-A and IV-B: to *simulate* tampering the authors blur with a 3x3 binomial filter and downsample by 2 "in order to destroy the CFA periodic correlations" - so resampling/downsampling kills the cue outright.
- Assumes a known periodic CFA lattice for the synthetic reference map (Eq. 55). Their own Nikon Coolpix 950 uses a four-filter CMYG CFA (Section IV) rather than RGB Bayer.
- No reference camera and no training for the EM itself. The *thresholds* are fitted empirically on non-CFA images (Section IV-B), and the CFA-algorithm-identification experiment uses PCA/LDA training (Section IV) - but that is a separate task from tamper detection.
- JPEG: survives only at very high quality. Section IV-B: accuracies "close to 100% for quality factors greater than 96 (out of 100)", degrading gracefully below; at quality 70 smooth hue is 56% and adaptive colour plane drops to 6%.

**Reported performance:**
- Section IV-B, CFA vs non-CFA classification averaged over 100 images at 0% false positives: bilinear 100%, bicubic 100%, smooth hue 100%, median 3x3 99%, median 5x5 97%, gradient-based 100%, adaptive colour plane 97%, variable number of gradients 100%.
- Section IV-B, additive white Gaussian noise: at SNR 18 dB, 76% for adaptive colour plane and 86% for variable number of gradients.
- Section IV-B, gamma correction with exponents 0.5 to 1.5 in steps of 0.1: accuracies "either 100% or 98%".
- Section IV, CFA-algorithm identification by LDA on the 24-D alpha vector, all 28 algorithm pairs, 90/10 split over 10 random splits: average testing accuracy 97%, minimum 88% (5x5 median vs adaptive colour plane).
- Section IV-C, commercial cameras (Canon Powershot A80 JPEG, Nikon D100 TIFF, Kodak DCS 14N TIFF, three images each): with 256x256 windows all windows correct except 2 of 748 in one Kodak image; with 512x512 windows all windows correct.
- Dataset for the main experiments (Section IV): 100 images, 50 at 512x512 and 50 at 1024x1024, cropped from twenty 1600x1200 Nikon Coolpix 950 TIFFs and twenty 3034x2024 Nikon D100 RAW files.

**Relevance to our training-free localizer on Korus:** This is the right foundation for the CFA cue - the EM is fully unsupervised and CPU-only, with no classifier trained on tampering. Korus is camera-native uncompressed TIFF at 1920x1080 from four consumer DSLRs, so demosaicing traces should be intact, which is exactly the regime where Section IV-C reported near-perfect window classification on real cameras. The catch is granularity: localisation here is by FFT of 256x256 or 512x512 windows of the posterior map (Sections IV-A, IV-C), far coarser than a per-pixel suspicion map, so a finer-grained variant (Ferrara 2012) is needed on top. For the recompression stress condition this cue is close to useless below JPEG quality ~96 (Section IV-B).

**Limitations the authors themselves state:** Section V: the approach is "vulnerable to counter-attack" - a tampered image can be resampled onto a CFA and re-interpolated, though this needs knowledge of the camera's CFA pattern and interpolation algorithm. Section III: the linear model is "perhaps overly simplistic when compared to the highly non-linear nature of most CFA interpolation algorithms", and inter-channel correlations are ignored. Section IV-B: accuracy decreases with JPEG quality because compression noise destroys the correlations.

**What the method computes:** Full step-by-step trace with file/line citations is in the dedicated section `## CFA reference implementation, step by step` below - this is the primary content for this entry, since the paper's own text was not reachable. Summary: green channel only -> fixed Laplacian-style prediction filter -> local variance of the prediction residual computed separately at CFA-acquired and CFA-interpolated pixel positions using a masked 7x7 Gaussian window -> a block statistic (product of acquired-position variances over product of interpolated-position variances) computed on Nb x Nb blocks -> a 2-component Gaussian mixture fitted in the log of that statistic, one component's mean forced to zero -> a log-likelihood-ratio map -> optional cumulation over Ns x Ns blocks-of-blocks -> 5x5 median filter -> logistic transform to a final probability map. Localization granularity is Nb x Nb pixels (Nb=2 used in the authors' own demo scripts is the finest possible, exactly one CFA period), which is dramatically finer than Popescu 2005's 256x256/512x512 window-FFT approach (see `popescu2005cfa` above), matching the paper's stated contribution of a "fine-grained" localization down to a "2x2 image block" (language visible in the retrieved abstract snippet, consistent with `getFeature.m` block size and with the `Nb` variable name "feature dimension" in `CFAloc.m`'s docstring).

**Assumptions and preconditions:**
- Requires the periodic CFA acquired/interpolated pixel structure to still be intact, i.e. camera-native, non-resampled imagery - the same fundamental precondition as Popescu 2005, since the whole feature is built on the asymmetry between acquired-phase and interpolated-phase local variance of a demosaicing residual (`getVarianceMap.m`, `getFeature.m`). INFERENCE: any resizing or resampling that breaks the 2x2 periodicity of the CFA lattice should destroy this cue for the same reason it destroys Popescu's cue - not verified from retrieved text for this specific paper, since the paper's own robustness section was not reachable.
- Needs the Bayer CFA phase (`Bayer`, a 2x2 0/1 matrix marking which of the 4 sub-lattice positions is the "acquired" green sample) as an explicit input to `CFAloc.m` (`CFAloc.m` line 1 signature, "Bayer: 2x2 Bayer pattern of green channel. It is assumed to be known"). The repository ships `GetCFASimple.m`, which auto-detects the phase by testing 4 candidate patterns and picking the one whose bilinear-CFA reconstruction best matches non-smooth 16x16 blocks (`GetCFASimple.m` full file) - so no manual camera-specific calibration is strictly required, but this auto-detection step is itself training-free/unsupervised (a per-image best-fit search over 4 hypotheses), not a learned classifier.
- No trained classifier or reference camera fingerprint anywhere in the pipeline - purely EM (`EMGaussianZM.m`) fit per image, fully consistent with our training-free/CPU-only requirement.
- JPEG and resizing robustness: `not verified from retrieved text` - the paper's own robustness experiments were not reachable this session (abstract only). INFERENCE: since the cue depends on a fixed spatial (not frequency-domain) residual-variance asymmetry at exact 2-pixel-period positions, it is plausibly at least as fragile to JPEG blocking/quantization noise and to any resampling as Popescu 2005's cue, but this is inference, not a reported result.

**Reported performance:** `not verified from retrieved text` - no numeric results, tables, or ROC curves were reachable (abstract only; abstract itself contains no numbers).

**Relevance to our training-free localizer on Korus:** This is very likely the best CFA-cue candidate for our pipeline, precisely because of the localization granularity: `getFeature.m`'s block statistic can be computed at Nb=2 (one CFA period = 2x2 pixels), and the demo scripts' own Ns=1 (`demo.m` line 7, `demo_Piva.m` line 26) means no forced block-of-blocks cumulation - so the output before the 5x5 median filter (`getMap.m` line 31, `CFAloc.m` line 45 `Nm=5`) is close to a genuine per-2x2-block suspicion map, several orders finer than Popescu's 256x256 windows. On Korus's uncompressed camera-native TIFFs the acquired/interpolated variance asymmetry this method relies on should be fully intact. It is CPU-only, unsupervised (EM per image, no training set), and produces a probability map directly usable as (or upsampled into) our per-pixel suspicion map. The main open risk for the recompression stress condition is `not verified from retrieved text` and should be treated as unproven until the paper's robustness section or an independent test is obtained.

**Limitations the authors themselves state:** `not verified from retrieved text` - the paper's discussion/limitations sections were not reachable this session (abstract only, and the abstract states only the positive contribution, not limitations).

## CFA reference implementation, step by step

Source: authors' own GPLv3 MATLAB code, fetched from `raw.githubusercontent.com/caomw/matlab-forensics/master/04 - Image Forgery Localization via Fine-Grained Analysis of CFA Artifacts/` this session (see Retrieval note above for provenance and why the accompanying PDF in that folder was not used). Entry point is `CFAloc.m`; every step below cites the specific file, and line numbers refer to the file as fetched this session (each file is short, single-purpose, and was read in full).

1. **Green-channel-only analysis.** `CFAloc.m` line 49: `im = image(:,:,2);` - only the green channel of the input image is processed. The red and blue channels are never touched by this pipeline. (Green is chosen because in a standard Bayer CFA it is sampled at 50% of pixels vs 25% for red/blue, giving denser acquired-vs-interpolated statistics - INFERENCE, not stated in the code comments.)

2. **Prediction filter (interpolation/demosaicing residual).** `prediction.m` lines 23-27. A single fixed 3x3 kernel is convolved over the *whole* green channel (both acquired and interpolated pixels alike, no phase-dependent filtering at this stage):
   ```
   Hpred = [ 0,    0.25,  0;
             0.25, -1,    0.25;
             0,    0.25,  0 ];
   pred_error = imfilter(double(im), double(Hpred), 'replicate');
   ```
   This is a discrete Laplacian: `pred_error(x,y) = 0.25*(N+S+E+W) - center`. Boundary handling is `'replicate'` (edge-pixel replication), applied uniformly - the asymmetry between acquired and interpolated pixel statistics is extracted downstream, not by using a different predictor per phase.

3. **Local variance of the prediction residual, split by CFA phase.** `getVarianceMap.m`.
   - Line 22: `pattern = kron(ones(dim(1)/2,dim(2)/2), Bayer)` tiles the 2x2 `Bayer` phase mask (caller-supplied, marks which of the 4 sub-lattice positions is "acquired") across the full image.
   - Lines 25-31: a fixed 7x7 checkerboard `mask` (alternating 1/0 in both dimensions) restricts an averaging window to taps that sit on the *same* 2-pixel-period sub-lattice as the center pixel.
   - Line 34: `window = gaussian_window().*mask` - the checkerboard mask is multiplied elementwise by a 7x7 isotropic Gaussian kernel (`gaussian_window.m` lines 24-29: window size 7, `sigma=1`, `meshgrid` over `[-2,2]` in steps of `4/6`, then `(1/(2*pi*sigma^2))*exp(-0.5*(x^2+y^2)/sigma^2)`).
   - Lines 35-37: `mc = sum(sum(window))`; `vc = 1 - sum(sum(window.^2))`; `window_mean = window./mc` - `mc` normalizes the window into a weighted local mean filter; `vc` (computed from the *unnormalized* Gaussian*mask weights) is used below as a bias-correction denominator for the variance estimate.
   - Lines 40-43 (acquired pixels): `acquired = im.*pattern`; local weighted mean and mean-of-squares are computed with `imfilter(...,'replicate')` using `window_mean`, each re-masked by `pattern`; `var_map_acquired = (sqmean - mean^2)/vc`.
   - Lines 46-49 (interpolated pixels): identical computation with `interpolated = im.*(1-pattern)` and mask `(1-pattern)`, giving `var_map_interpolated`.
   - Line 52: `var_map = var_map_acquired + var_map_interpolated` - since every pixel belongs to exactly one phase (its own value is zero in the other phase's map), this sum simply assigns each pixel the local variance of the prediction residual computed only over same-phase neighbours within its 7x7 (Gaussian-weighted, checkerboard-subsampled) neighbourhood.
   - Net effect: `var_map(x,y)` is a windowed local-variance estimate of the demosaicing-residual, evaluated separately (never mixing phases) for whichever of "CFA-acquired" or "CFA-interpolated" the pixel `(x,y)` happens to be.

4. **Block statistic ("proposed feature").** `getFeature.m`.
   - Line 24: `pattern = kron(ones(Nb/2,Nb/2),Bayer)` - the same 2x2 phase mask, now tiled to `Nb x Nb` blocks (`Nb` is the caller-supplied "feature dimension" / block size, must be even).
   - Line 26: `func = @(sigma) prod(sigma(logical(pattern))) / prod(sigma(not(logical(pattern))))` - within each `Nb x Nb` block of `var_map`, take the product of all values at *acquired*-phase positions divided by the product of all values at *interpolated*-phase positions.
   - Line 28: `statistics = blkproc(map,[Nb Nb],func)` applies this over non-overlapping `Nb x Nb` blocks of the image, producing one scalar statistic per block - this is the base spatial resolution of the whole method. With `Nb=2` (used in both demo scripts, see below) each block is exactly one CFA period (2x2 pixels), so the "product" degenerates to a single acquired-variance value over a single interpolated-variance value - the finest possible grid. With `Nb=8` (also used in the demos) each block pools 16 CFA periods for a more robust but 4x coarser-per-axis statistic.
   - Lines 30-31: `NaN -> 1`, `Inf -> 0` cleanup.

5. **Statistical model: 2-component Gaussian mixture on log(statistic), one mean forced to zero.** `MoGEstimationZM.m` + `EMGaussianZM.m`.
   - `MoGEstimationZM.m` lines 31-34: non-positive/NaN statistics are floored/removed, then `data = log(statistics(:))` - the EM is fit in the log domain of the block ratio statistic, and `+-Inf`/`NaN` entries are dropped.
   - `MoGEstimationZM.m` line 26: `tol = 1e-3`, `max_iter = 500`.
   - `EMGaussianZM.m` models `x ~ alpha*N(0,v1) + (1-alpha)*N(mu2,v2)` - a 2-component Gaussian mixture where **one component's mean is fixed at exactly 0** (`EMGaussianZM.m` line 19 doc comment, and the E/M update in lines 37-45 never re-estimates a mean for the first component). Initialization (lines 25-28): `alpha=0.5`, `mu2=mean(x)`, `v2=var(x)`, `v1=v2/10`. Standard EM loop (lines 33-46): E-step computes responsibilities `alpha1, alpha2` from the two Gaussian densities; M-step updates `alpha`, `v1` (weighted second moment about 0), `mu2`, `v2` (weighted variance about `mu2`); loop terminates when `|alpha - alpha_old| <= tol` or after `max_iter` iterations (a warning is printed via `display(...)` if it hits the cap without converging, line 48-50).
   - `MoGEstimationZM.m` lines 42-46 repackage the outputs: `mu = [mu2; 0]`, `sigma = sqrt([v2; v1])`, `mix_perc = [1-alpha; alpha]` - index 1 is always the free-mean component, index 2 is always the zero-mean-forced component.
   - Which physical hypothesis (authentic vs tampered block) each component is meant to represent is stated in the paper's own text (not reachable this session - `not verified from retrieved text`). INFERENCE: since the block feature is a ratio of acquired-position to interpolated-position variance, an authentic CFA-interpolated block should show a stable, non-zero systematic log-ratio (interpolation smooths the interpolated positions relative to the acquired ones), while a block whose CFA correlation has been destroyed (tampered/resampled) should trend toward ratio approx 1, i.e. log-ratio approx 0 - so the zero-mean-forced component plausibly models the *tampered* hypothesis and the free-mean component the *authentic* one. This mapping is an inference from the code structure, not confirmed by the retrieved paper text.

6. **Log-likelihood map.** `loglikelihood.m` lines 33-42.
   ```
   mu1 = mu(2);     % = 0, the zero-mean-forced component
   mu2 = mu(1);     % the free-mean component
   sigma1 = sigma(2);
   sigma2 = sigma(1);
   L = log(sigma1) - log(sigma2) ...
       - 0.5*( (log(stat)-mu2).^2/sigma2^2 - (log(stat)-mu1).^2/sigma1^2 );
   ```
   Algebraically this equals `log N(log(stat); mu2_free, sigma2_free) - log N(log(stat); 0, sigma1_zero)`, i.e. the log-likelihood ratio of the free-mean component over the zero-mean-forced component, evaluated per block. `NaN/Inf/negative` statistics are floored/capped first (lines 25-31, using `1e-320`/`1e304` as numeric guards for the log).

7. **Cumulation over larger neighbourhoods and smoothing.** `getMap.m`.
   - Line 27: `log_L_cum = blkproc(L,[Ns Ns],@(x) sum(x(:)))` - sums the per-block log-likelihoods over non-overlapping `Ns x Ns` groups of blocks (`Ns` = "number of blocks to cumulate", a `CFAloc.m` input parameter). Both demo scripts use `Ns=1` (`demo.m` line 7, `demo_Piva.m` line 26), i.e. no extra cumulation beyond the base `Nb x Nb` block grid.
   - Line 31: `map = medfilt2(log_L_cum,[Nm Nm],'symmetric')` - a median filter of size `Nm x Nm` is applied to the (possibly cumulated) log-likelihood map, with symmetric boundary extension. `Nm = 5` is hardcoded in `CFAloc.m` line 45 and is not exposed as a caller argument.

8. **Final probability map.** `CFAloc.m` lines 72-74:
   ```
   expMap = exp(map);
   probMap = 1./(expMap+1);
   map = probMap;
   ```
   This is the logistic/sigmoid transform `probMap = 1/(1+exp(L))` applied to the filtered, cumulated log-likelihood-ratio map, turning it into a bounded `[0,1]` per-block value that `CFAloc.m`'s own docstring and the paper's abstract snippet call a tampering probability. Output spatial resolution is `(image_height/Nb/Ns) x (image_width/Nb/Ns)` blocks (before any upsampling back to pixel space, which the reference code does not itself perform - `FixMapOutputs.m`, a separate batch-postprocessing script found in the same folder, only re-applies this same sigmoid transform to older saved outputs and does not upsample either).

9. **Bayer-phase auto-detection (used by the demo scripts, not by `CFAloc.m` itself).** `GetCFASimple.m`, full file.
   - Line 8: 4 candidate CFA phase arrangements are hard-coded as `SmallCFAList`.
   - Lines 12-42: for each candidate, the image is masked into R/G/B sub-lattices matching that candidate, bilinear-CFA-reconstructed (`bilinInterp.m`, referenced but not read in full this session), and compared against the true image over 16x16 blocks (`W1=16`); only "non-smooth" blocks (per-block std > `StdThresh=5`) are used to score reconstruction error.
   - Lines 45-51: the candidate with minimum mean reconstruction error over non-smooth blocks is selected as the Bayer phase (`bayer=CFAOut`); an auxiliary uniformity statistic `F1` is also returned but not used by `CFAloc.m`.
   - This step is itself training-free (a per-image, per-candidate best-fit search over exactly 4 hypotheses, no learned model), consistent with the project's constraints.

10. **Parameters actually exercised by the authors' own demo scripts.** `demo.m` lines 5-7 and `demo_Piva.m` lines 21-29: `Nb = [2, 8]` (run twice, once at each block size), `Ns = 1` (no cumulation), and in `demo_Piva.m` an explicit `bayer = [0,1; 1,0]` phase (a synthetic-image test on `flowers.tiff`/`flowers-tampered.tiff` and JPEG-compressed `garden.jpg`/`garden-tampered.jpg` pairs shipped in the same folder - the presence of a JPEG test pair is suggestive that the authors did test JPEG robustness, but no numeric result from running it was reachable this session, so this remains `not verified from retrieved text`).

Steps not fully traced: `bilinInterp.m` (the bilinear CFA reconstruction used only inside the auxiliary `GetCFASimple.m` phase-detector, not inside the core `CFAloc.m` pipeline) was fetched but not read line-by-line, since it is not part of the core localization algorithm.

**What the method computes:**
1. *Signal model.* Section 3, Eq. 1: `f_n(x,y) = f(x,y) + n(x,y)`, where `f` is the uncorrupted signal and `n` is zero-mean additive white Gaussian noise whose variance sigma^2 is a **piecewise-constant function of position** (can differ region to region).
2. *Wavelet analysis.* Section 3.1: a single one-level wavelet decomposition of the (grayscale) image is performed. Section 4 (experiments): Daubechies wavelet `db8` was used.
3. *Tiling.* Section 3.2: the `HH1` sub-band (diagonal detail at the finest resolution) is tiled with non-overlapping `R x R` blocks `B_i`; blocks must be smaller than the corrupted region to be detected; total block count `r = floor(M/R) * floor(N/R)` for an `M x N` image. Section 3.2 also notes an operator can instead manually supply an ROI, optionally obtained from another localization method.
4. *Per-block noise estimation.* Section 3.3, Eq. 2: the classic wavelet median estimator, `sigma_hat = median(|HH1|) / 0.6745` (i.e. `MAD(HH1)/0.6745`), applied independently inside each block. Cited from Donoho & Johnstone's wavelet-shrinkage work (ref. [12] in the paper) rather than original to this paper - the paper's contribution is applying it locally per block and then segmenting on it, not the estimator itself.
5. *Region merging.* Section 3.4: a standard region-growing/merging segmentation is run over the block grid using `sigma_hat_i` as the sole homogeneity feature - each block starts as its own labeled region; neighboring regions are merged if `|sigma_hat_i - sigma_hat_j| < T` (a single similarity threshold `T`), each merge re-estimates the new region's `sigma_hat`, and the process repeats until no further merges are possible. Output: a label map partitioning the image into connected regions of approximately homogeneous noise standard deviation - **this is a noise-level segmentation, not directly a tampering probability map**; Section 5 explicitly states a human must interpret the output ("a human interpretation of the output of the method is necessary").
6. *Parameters used in the qualitative demo (Section 4, Figs. 1-3):* test images 1200x800, block size `M=N=40` (i.e. `R=40`), `T=1`, Daubechies `db8`, tested at injected-noise standard deviations sigma = 1, 3, 5, 7, 10, 15.
7. *Quantitative noise-estimator-only evaluation (Section 4, Figs. 4-7):* 1000 randomly chosen blocks (one per image) from the green channel of 1000 color images sized 1600x1200, additive Gaussian noise at sigma = 0, 2.5, 5, 7.5, 10, 12.5, 15, 17.5, 20, tested at block sizes 16x16, 32x32, 64x64, 128x128, each under 4 conditions: uncorrupted-format ("pure"), JPEG 95, JPEG 90, JPEG 70 (noise added before JPEG compression in the JPEG conditions). This evaluates step 4 (the block noise estimator) in isolation, not the full merging pipeline.
8. *Runtime.* Section 5: the unoptimized MATLAB experimental version, with `M=N=40`, `T=1`, took an average of 25 s per 1200x800 grayscale image on a 2.1 GHz CPU / 512 MB RAM, with most time spent in the region-merging step.

**Assumptions and preconditions:**
- Assumes additive white Gaussian noise with piecewise-constant variance (Section 3, Eq. 1) - a splicing/copy-paste scenario where the pasted region carries a different noise level than the host image, or where a forger has added local noise to mask other tampering traces (Section 1).
- Grayscale by design in this paper; Section 5 states the method "has been concerned with gray-level images" and suggests (untested here) per-channel application to RGB as future work.
- No reference camera, no training, purely per-image blind estimation - fits the training-free/CPU-only requirement directly.
- Section 5 explicitly flags a failure mode relevant to false positives: authentic images can themselves contain regions with genuinely different noise variance (e.g., different exposure/ISO/scene content), and the method will flag these as "inconsistent" - the authors recommend using it "as a supplement to other forgery detection methods rather then a standalone forgery detector."
- Section 5: the method "is not able to find the corrupted regions when the noise degradation is very small (sigma < 2)" - i.e. it needs the added/spliced noise discrepancy to be at least a few grey levels of standard deviation to be detectable, which the authors state is not a significant limitation for their target use case (detecting the deliberately-added noise that forgers use to mask other traces, which tends to be non-trivial in magnitude).
- JPEG robustness: **explicitly tested** at the noise-estimator level (step 7 above, Figs. 4-7) at JPEG 95/90/70. Visual inspection of the plots (Figs. 4-7, all four block sizes) this session: the "pure", JPEG 95 and JPEG 90 panels track the true-sigma diagonal closely at all four block sizes; the JPEG 70 panel shows a visibly larger downward bias (systematic underestimation of sigma) and wider error bars than the other three conditions, most pronounced at the smaller block sizes (16x16, 32x32) and visibly less pronounced at the larger block sizes (64x64, 128x128) - this is a direct visual read of Figs. 4-7 and no numeric bias values are given in the text itself, so treat the magnitude as qualitative only.
- Resizing/rescaling robustness: `not verified from retrieved text` - not tested or discussed anywhere in this paper.

**Reported performance:** No single scalar accuracy/F1/AUC is reported anywhere in the paper; results are qualitative (Figs. 1-3: example segmentations at various injected sigma) and the quantitative part (Figs. 4-7) reports only the noise-*estimator's* mean and standard deviation against true sigma, read graphically rather than tabulated. Section 5 reported runtime: 25 s/image, `1200x800`, 2.1 GHz / 512 MB RAM, unoptimized MATLAB. No comparison table against Popescu-Farid ref. [13] is given beyond the qualitative claim in Section 5 that "the proposed method uses a more precise estimation of noise level" and that ref. [13]'s dependence on a known/estimated original-image kurtosis "brings numerical errors and decreases the performance" (not quantified).

**Relevance to our training-free localizer on Korus:** Directly usable as an additional, independent cue: fully blind, CPU-only, no reference camera or training set, and it targets exactly the "splice with mismatched noise floor" scenario that CFA/demosaicing cues do not cover (a well-executed splice can preserve CFA correlation locally if resampled back onto a matching lattice, but matching the exact sensor noise level of the host image is a separate, harder task for a forger). On Korus's uncompressed camera-native TIFFs this should work close to the tested "pure" condition (best case in Figs. 4-7). The output is a noise-level segmentation map, not a calibrated per-pixel suspicion score, so turning it into our suspicion map needs a design choice (INFERENCE: e.g. treat "distance of each region's sigma_hat from the image-wide mode of sigma_hat" as the suspicion score) - the paper itself does not propose this. The stated sigma < 2 detection floor (Section 5) and the explicit false-positive risk from naturally heterogeneous authentic-image noise (Section 5) both need to be taken seriously for Korus, whose forgeries are camera-native and may have very small or zero genuine noise-level mismatch, in which case this cue would simply not fire. For the recompression stress condition, this paper's own data (Figs. 4-7, JPEG 70 panels) suggests degraded but not destroyed performance, unlike Popescu 2005's CFA cue which the retrieved text there reported as failing hard below quality ~96.

**Limitations the authors themselves state:** Section 5, verbatim substance: (1) authentic images can contain genuinely inconsistent-noise regions and get flagged, so "a human interpretation of the output... is necessary" and the method is recommended "as a supplement... rather then a standalone forgery detector"; (2) cannot detect corrupted regions when the noise discrepancy is small (sigma < 2); (3) the block-merging step is the main computational cost and the implementation was not optimized; (4) the paper only addresses grayscale images, leaving RGB extension (e.g. per-channel application) unexplored; (5) method parameters (`R`, `T`) were "determined experimentally" for a size/noise-level tradeoff and are not adaptively chosen by the algorithm itself.

**What the method computes:**
1. *Kurtosis/variance relation (Section 3.2, Eq. 3-4).* For `y = x + n` with `x` a natural-image signal and `n` zero-mean Gaussian noise independent of `x`, additivity of cumulants gives `kappa(y) * sigma(y)^4 = kappa(x) * sigma(x)^4`, i.e. `kappa(y) = kappa(x) * (sigma(y)^2 - sigma(n)^2)^2 / sigma(y)^4` (IJCV Eq. 3). The same relation holds for 1-D projections `w^T y` of a high-dimensional patch vector onto unit vectors `w` corresponding to band-pass filters (IJCV Eq. 4), and is shown (IJCV Section 3.2, via the Lyapunov CLT, Appendix 1) to approximately hold even for non-Gaussian i.i.d. pixel-domain noise, because any i.i.d. noise becomes approximately Gaussian after linear band-pass mixing.
2. *Projection kurtosis concentration (Section 3.3-3.4, the paper's core empirical/theoretical claim).* The kurtosis of natural-image patches projected onto many different band-pass filters (DCT, PCA, ICA, Haar wavelet, or random symmetric bases, all tested at 8x8) clusters tightly around one positive constant, rather than varying freely per filter (IJCV Fig. 3 left panel, 10,000 patches from 200 Van Hateren images). IJCV Section 3.4 gives a theoretical justification via the Gaussian Scale Mixture (GSM) image model: Claim 1 (proved in Appendix 2) states that for a zero-mean GSM vector `x`, the projection kurtosis `kappa(w^T x) = 3*var(z)/E[z]^2` is *independent of the projection direction w*, where `z` is the GSM's positive latent scaling variable - this constant-across-filters property is exactly "kurtosis concentration."
3. *Global noise-variance estimator, closed form (Section 4.1, Eq. 9-11 - this is the paper's headline contribution).* Using `K` band-pass filters of unit L2 norm, measure the per-channel kurtosis `kappa_tilde_k` and variance `sigma_tilde_k^2` of the noisy image's filter responses. Because the noise-free kurtosis `kappa_k` is approximately constant `= kappa` across channels (step 2), Eq. 9 gives `sqrt(kappa_tilde_k) ~= sqrt(kappa) * (sigma_tilde_k^2 - sigma^2)/sigma_tilde_k^2`. Rather than solving this exactly (which is over-determined and unstable under real deviations from perfect concentration), the paper minimizes the sum of squared residuals `L(sqrt(kappa), sigma^2) = sum_k [ sqrt(kappa_tilde_k) - sqrt(kappa)*(sigma_tilde_k^2-sigma^2)/sigma_tilde_k^2 ]^2` (Eq. 10). This has a unique **closed-form** minimizer (Eq. 11, derived in Appendix 3):
   ```
   sqrt(kappa) = [ <sqrt(kappa_tilde_k)>_k * <1/sigma_tilde_k^4>_k  -  <sqrt(kappa_tilde_k)/sigma_tilde_k^2>_k * <1/sigma_tilde_k^2>_k ]
                 / [ <1/sigma_tilde_k^4>_k - <1/sigma_tilde_k^2>_k^2 ]
   sigma^2 = ( 1 / <1/sigma_tilde_k^2>_k ) * ( 1 - <sqrt(kappa_tilde_k)>_k / sqrt(kappa) )_+
   ```
   where `<.>_k` denotes averaging over the `K` channels and `(x)_+ = max(x,0)`. The `sigma^2` estimate is (per the authors' own reading of the formula) a harmonic mean of the per-channel projected variances, modulated by the kurtosis-derived factor - the harmonic-mean structure is stated to give robustness against large per-channel outliers (Section 4.1, discussion after Eq. 11).
4. *Filter choice (Section 3.3, 4.1).* Both deterministic 8x8 DCT AC filters and random 8x8 band-pass filters (symmetric-orthogonalized random matrices, same construction as Bethge 2006) were tested; random filters show measurably stronger kurtosis concentration (tighter clustering in Fig. 3 left) and give better estimator accuracy at high PSNR (Table 2), at the cost of higher estimate variance. The reference implementation used 63 filters of size 8x8 in the main experiments (Section 4.4, Table 1).
5. *Extension to non-Gaussian and multiplicative noise (Section 4.3, Eq. 13-16).* Because the estimator only needs approximate Gaussianity of the *band-pass-filtered* noise (guaranteed by the CLT argument in step 1 for any i.i.d. pixel-domain noise), the same closed-form estimator can recover the scale parameter of additive Laplacian noise (`s = sqrt(sigma^2)`) or, after a log-transform (`log y = log x + log z`, Eq. 15), the shape/rate parameters of multiplicative Gamma noise (via digamma/trigamma inversion, Eq. 16).
6. *Local noise estimation, accelerated with integral images (Section 5, the step that actually enables per-pixel localization).* The raw moments needed for Eq. 2 (variance and kurtosis from first-to-fourth raw moments) are computed inside a sliding rectangular window `Omega^k_(i,j)` around every pixel `(i,j)`, per band-pass channel `k`, via **integral images** of the 1st-4th power of each filter response (Eq. 17: 4 corner lookups per window per moment order per channel) - this reduces a naive `O(MNK)` sliding-window cost (`M`=window size, `N`=image size, `K`=#channels) to `O(NK)`, independent of window size (Section 5, algorithm summary numbered list before Section 5.1). Reported speedup: an 800x600-ish image (`600x800` stated) drops from >600 s to 8 s with the integral-image acceleration (Section 5.1, footnote 8 area of text).
7. *Region-splicing detection pipeline (Section 6).* Local noise variance is estimated at every pixel by **averaging the local estimator's output over several window sizes** `n x n` for `n` in `{2,4,8,10,12}`, using 63 random 8x8 band-pass filters (Section 6, first paragraph) - the multi-window averaging is explicitly to trade off estimation precision (large windows) against localization of rapid changes (small windows). The resulting per-pixel local-variance map is then **segmented into regions of significantly different variance**, small isolated regions are removed, and remaining regions are connected/smoothed with mathematical morphology (Section 6, same paragraph) - this final segmentation/morphology step is described only at this level of detail; no explicit algorithm/threshold formula for "segment into distinct regions" is given beyond this prose (`not verified from retrieved text` for the exact segmentation rule - unlike Mahdian & Saic 2009's explicit region-merging pseudocode, this paper does not spell out the equivalent step as pseudocode).
8. *Quantitative splicing-detection evaluation (Section 6.2).* 100 UCID images; forged by picking a random pair, cutting a random rectangular region from one, adding white Gaussian noise of a controlled level to it, and pasting into the other. Detection accuracy = fraction of tampered pixels correctly flagged; false positive rate = fraction of untampered pixels flagged. ROC curves (Fig. 10) reported vs. (a) local SNR of the added noise at fixed 16% region size, (b) spliced-region size (5%/10%/15%/20%) at fixed 20 dB local SNR, (c) JPEG quality of the final forged image at fixed noise/size settings - **JPEG robustness is explicitly tested** (Fig. 10, right panel) and, per Section 6.2's own summary, "performances deteriorate with lower noise variances, smaller regions, or low JPEG qualities."

**Assumptions and preconditions:**
- Noise model: additive, signal-independent, i.i.d., and (at minimum after band-pass filtering) approximately Gaussian (Section 7, explicit list of the 4 assumptions: "(1) additive, (2) signal independent, (3) independent, and (4) Gaussian distributed"); the paper itself states these can be "relaxed to certain extend" (non-Gaussian/multiplicative cases, step 5 above) but not eliminated.
- Assumes spliced regions carry a genuinely different intrinsic noise variance than the host image (Section 6, Section 7) - the same fundamental precondition as Mahdian & Saic 2009, and the paper explicitly compares against and outperforms that method (Table 5: RMSE of estimated local noise std vs ground truth, our method 3.17/6.18/4.12 on stripe/annulus/checkerboard synthetic patterns vs Mahdian & Saic's 7.69/12.76/9.89 - "Bold values indicate better performance").
- No trained classifier, no reference camera - closed-form optimization per image, fully training-free/CPU-only, consistent with our project constraints. Runtime figures given (unoptimized MATLAB, 2.4 GHz/4 GB RAM): global estimator ~3 s for an 800x600 image (Section 4.4.1); local estimator with integral-image acceleration 8 s for 600x800 vs >600 s without it (Section 5.1).
- JPEG robustness: **explicitly tested end-to-end for the splicing-detection pipeline**, not just the estimator (Section 6.2, Fig. 10 right panel) - performance falls off at low JPEG quality; Section 6.1's qualitative comparison (Fig. 9) shows the method "fails to detect the entire tampered regions" at JPEG quality 65, attributed explicitly to "heavy JPEG compression affects most the high-frequency bands of the image, where the noise resides," reducing the noise-level difference between spliced and host regions - directly relevant to our recompression stress condition.
- Resizing robustness: `not verified from retrieved text` - not tested or discussed in either paper.
- False-positive precondition/limitation stated explicitly (Section 6.1): when run on the 180 *original, untampered* images of the Columbia Uncompressed Splicing Dataset, the method found spurious "spliced regions of sufficient size" in 5 of them, "mainly due to the variation of local noise variances within these untampered images" (e.g. sky vs. textured foliage) - i.e. a quantified false-positive rate of 5/180 (2.8%) on genuinely authentic camera images under the paper's own detection settings.

**Reported performance:**
- Global noise-variance estimator (additive Gaussian), Table 1: on Kodak/UCID/Van Hateren datasets at PSNR 50/40/30/25/20 dB, "Ours" achieves the mean PSNR closest to ground truth in most rows (e.g. Kodak 20 dB: Ours 20.07 (0.12) vs ground truth 20, vs Zoran&Weiss 20.29 (0.32), vs MAD 19.83 (0.15)) and is described as "comparable" to the best competing method (Liu et al. 2012) while significantly beating Zoran & Weiss (2009), "particularly for low noise levels" (Section 4.4.1).
- Runtime comparison (Section 4.4.1): for an 800x600 image, "our method and the method of Liu et al. (2012) take on average about 3 s, the method of Zoran and Weiss (2009) takes about 10 s, whereas it takes the MAD estimator about 0.5 s."
- Non-Gaussian/multiplicative noise (Tables 3-4): for Laplacian scale `s` and Gamma shape `alpha` at several ground-truth values, "Ours" is closest to ground truth in most but not all rows (e.g. Table 3, s=10: Ours 10.29 vs ground truth 10; Table 4, alpha=25: Ours 28.44, closer than LTO's 30.22 or MAD's 30.32 but not exact).
- Local noise estimation RMSE (Table 5, quoted above): our method beats Mahdian & Saic (2009) by roughly 2x lower RMSE on all three synthetic spatial noise patterns tested.
- Splicing localization, qualitative (Section 6.1): 180 Columbia dataset forgeries and Worth1000.com contest images, method "provides a higher level of accuracy [than Mahdian & Saic 2009 and Pan et al. 2011] because the region is determined to the level of pixels" (no single scalar accuracy number given for this qualitative comparison).
- Splicing localization, quantitative (Section 6.2): ROC curves only (Fig. 10, three panels for noise level, region size, JPEG quality) - no single AUC/accuracy number is stated in the extracted text; "results show that the overall detection accuracies for higher noise variances or larger regions are better."

**Relevance to our training-free localizer on Korus:** This is a substantially stronger candidate than Mahdian & Saic 2009 for the same noise-inconsistency cue family: it is CPU-only, training-free (a per-image closed-form optimization, not an EM iteration or a learned model), explicitly benchmarked to beat Mahdian & Saic 2009 by roughly 2x on local-variance RMSE (Table 5) and to produce pixel-level (not block-level) splicing localization via the integral-image-accelerated multi-window-averaged local estimator (Section 6). On Korus's uncompressed camera-native TIFFs this should be close to the paper's own uncompressed-image test conditions (Columbia dataset, also uncompressed TIFF, Section 6.1) - a favorable match. The explicit JPEG-quality ROC sweep (Fig. 10 right) and the JPEG-65 qualitative failure case (Fig. 9, Section 6.1) are directly usable evidence for our recompression stress condition, and are more informative than what we could extract for the CFA papers. The authors' own stated false-positive rate on genuine images (5/180, Section 6.1) and their explicit warning that texture/smooth-region noise heterogeneity within a single authentic photo is "the major source of false detections" (Section 7) are important cautions for fusing this cue on Korus, whose forgeries are drawn from real photographic scenes with exactly that kind of heterogeneity. Section 7 also flags, as a direct counter-forensic risk, that a forger could use this same closed-form estimator to measure and compensate the noise-variance mismatch before releasing a forgery - worth keeping in mind if this cue is used as a hard filter rather than one signal fused with others.

**Limitations the authors themselves state:** Section 7, itemized: (1) relies on the spliced region having a genuinely different intrinsic noise variance from the host - fails when the difference is insignificant, "one case in point is when the tampered image underwent heavy JPEG compression"; (2) assumes intrinsic noise variance is spatially uniform within the untampered image, which "may not hold for images with large regions with distinct texture and smooth regions... or large regions of saturated pixels" - stated as "the major source of false detections of our current method"; (3) noise-level inconsistency is "only one aspect" usable to expose splicing, and the method itself "provides an easy approach for forged images to avoid being detected... by first estimating and compensating the difference in noise variances" - an explicit counter-forensics vulnerability, with the authors recommending combination with other cues; (4) the noise model's four assumptions (additive, signal-independent, independent, Gaussian) are only partially relaxable, and extending to spatially/spectrally *correlated* noise (a full local covariance estimate rather than a scalar variance) is left as future work.

**What the method computes:**
1. *Splicebuster's own stated approach (verbatim from the GRIP project page abstract, `grip.unina.it/multimedia-forensics/image-forgery-detection`, fetched this session):* "We propose a new feature-based algorithm to detect image splicings without any prior information. Local features are computed from the co-occurrence of image residuals and used to extract synthetic feature parameters. Splicing and host images are assumed to be characterized by different parameters. These are learned by the image itself through the expectation-maximization algorithm together with the segmentation in genuine and spliced parts. A supervised version of the algorithm is also proposed. Preliminary results on a wide range of test images are very encouraging, showing that a limited-size, but meaningful, learning set may be sufficient for reliable splicing localization." This confirms the *default* Splicebuster mode is **blind/unsupervised per-image**: an EM algorithm run fresh on the single test image itself estimates two sets of feature-distribution parameters (presumably one for "host" pixels, one for "spliced" pixels) jointly with a genuine/spliced segmentation, with no external training set required for this default mode - exactly matching our training-free constraint. A "supervised version... is also proposed" as an explicit alternative, implying the paper itself distinguishes an unsupervised default from an optional supervised variant.
2. *Shared residual/co-occurrence feature pipeline, in full technical detail (from arXiv:1703.04615 Section II, "Residual-based Local Descriptors" - directly read; this is the SPAM/SRM-family feature construction that the arXiv paper states has "proven successful" for forgery detection and cites Splicebuster among the works establishing this):*
   - **(a) Noise residual extraction.** A high-pass filter is applied to extract fine image detail. The arXiv paper's own worked example (chosen "out of the 39 [filter] models proposed in" the SRM steganalysis paper of Fridrich & Kodovsky) uses a 4-tap 1-D linear filter with weights `w = [1, -3, 3, -1]`, applied along one direction and again on the image transpose to cover the orthogonal direction ("augment the available data" via horizontal/vertical invariance). **This specific 4-tap filter is the arXiv paper's illustrative example from the general SRM framework, not independently confirmed as Splicebuster's own literal filter** - `not verified from retrieved text` whether Splicebuster's WIFS paper uses this exact filter or a different one (e.g., a 3x3 kernel, given the GRIP thumbnail/teaser imagery suggests a spatial rather than 1-D residual, but this is INFERENCE only).
   - **(b) Scalar quantization.** The real-valued (or high-resolution integer) residual is quantized to a small odd number of levels `L` (odd so that 0 is always a representable output), with quantization step `Delta`. The arXiv paper's worked example sets `L=3`, `Delta=4.5` (Section II) - again the paper's own chosen illustrative values, not confirmed as Splicebuster's specific numbers.
   - **(c) Co-occurrence computation.** This is described as "the core step of the procedure" (Section II) - it captures high-order dependencies among residuals, i.e. recurring local micropatterns. The worked example computes co-occurrences over `N=4` residual pixels in a row, "both along and across the filter direction," giving two co-occurrence matrices of `L^N = 3^4 = 81` entries each (`N=4` pixels, each independently quantized to one of `L=3` levels).
   - **(d) Histogram / feature formation.** Counting co-occurrences yields a histogram that is the final feature vector; "neglecting symmetries, our final feature has length equal to 162" (i.e. `2 x 81`, from the two co-occurrence matrices in step c). This 162-D histogram-of-co-occurrences vector is the per-image (or, when computed on sliding patches, per-patch) descriptor.
   - **(e) Decision/classification.** In the arXiv paper's own baseline ("SRM+SVM"), this 162-D feature vector, computed per-patch across a labeled dataset, feeds a **linear SVM classifier trained on labeled pristine/manipulated patches** (Section II, last line: "The final classification phase is performed by a linear SVM classifier") - this is a supervised, trained pipeline, used by the arXiv paper to benchmark its proposed CNN recasting (Table II: accuracy on median filtering, Gaussian blurring, additive noise, resizing, and JPEG re-compression detection, at several parameter settings, comparing Bayar2016's CNN, SRM+SVM, and the paper's own proposed CNN). **This differs from Splicebuster's own stated default (step 1 above), which performs unsupervised per-image EM instead of a globally-trained SVM.** The paper does still demonstrate genuinely per-image localization heat maps with the SRM+SVM feature (Fig. 6, splicing and copy-move examples, "SRM+SVM heat map" panel) by applying the trained SVM in a sliding-window/patch modality over one test image - but the SVM itself was trained on a labeled multi-camera dataset (Section IV, "9 devices... 6 devices to form the training set, 3... testing set"), so this specific experiment is not training-free even though the underlying *feature* is unsupervised to compute.
3. *What was not retrieved and must be flagged as unverified for Splicebuster specifically:* the exact mathematical form of Splicebuster's own EM step (number of mixture components, whether a Gaussian mixture, dimensionality reduction such as PCA before EM, the Mahalanobis-distance or likelihood-ratio form of the final per-pixel/per-block anomaly score, block/patch size used for the sliding local co-occurrence computation, and the exact residual filter and quantization parameters used in the WIFS paper) are all `not verified from retrieved text` - none of this was recoverable from the GRIP abstract or the arXiv paper, and the WIFS paper itself was not obtained.

**Assumptions and preconditions:**
- Per the recovered abstract, Splicebuster requires **no prior information** about the tampering (no user-supplied ROI, no known camera, no reference images) and its default mode requires **no training set** - it is described as learning its two parameter sets "by the image itself" via EM, which is exactly the training-free, per-image, CPU-only profile our project needs, *if* the EM/segmentation step is as lightweight as the closed-form or iterative-but-unsupervised methods seen in the other papers in this file. Concrete runtime was not found - `not verified from retrieved text`.
- The underlying feature (residual + quantize + co-occurrence + histogram) is a **texture/micropattern descriptor of the noise residual**, conceptually adjacent to (but distinct in mechanism from) the CFA cue (Popescu 2005, Ferrara 2012) and the noise-variance cues (Mahdian & Saic 2009, Lyu 2014): rather than measuring a single scalar (variance) or a periodic correlation (CFA lattice), it captures the *joint statistical texture* of neighbouring high-pass residual values, which INFERENCE suggests could be sensitive to a broader range of processing-history mismatches (different denoising, different demosaicing algorithm, different sensor pattern) than a pure noise-variance or pure-CFA cue - but this breadth-of-sensitivity claim is not confirmed from retrieved Splicebuster text, only plausible from the general SPAM/SRM steganalysis literature's known strength at detecting diverse local processing artifacts (arXiv paper's own Table I: the same feature family is shown, in the arXiv paper's own experiments, to be effective across median filtering, Gaussian blur, AWGN, resizing, and JPEG re-compression detection tasks - though again, that is the arXiv paper's SVM-based classifier, not Splicebuster's unsupervised localizer).
- JPEG and resizing robustness for **Splicebuster itself**: `not verified from retrieved text`. The related arXiv paper (Table I, Table II) does test the underlying residual+co-occurrence feature family's sensitivity *as a manipulation detector* to JPEG compression (quality factors 70/80/90) and resizing (scale 1.01/1.125/1.5), finding (Table II, "SRM+SVM" columns) high accuracy at JPEG 70 (99.86% at large training set) degrading somewhat at JPEG 90 (90.90%), and high accuracy at resize scale 1.5 (99.99%) degrading at scale 1.01 (96.01% at small training set, 95.11% at large) - but this is the feature's ability to *detect that JPEG compression or resizing happened at all* to a whole patch, which is a different question from Splicebuster's actual task (*localizing a spliced region* via inconsistency in this feature between regions), so these numbers are only indirectly suggestive and are flagged as such rather than treated as Splicebuster's own robustness numbers.

**Reported performance:** For Splicebuster itself: `not verified from retrieved text` - no accuracy, F1, AUC, or dataset-size numbers were recoverable from the GRIP abstract (which says only "preliminary results on a wide range of test images are very encouraging," with no numbers) or from any other reachable source this session. For the related arXiv paper's own SRM+SVM baseline (not Splicebuster's actual algorithm, but the same feature family, supervised binary manipulation-classification task, Table II): e.g. median filtering 3x3 kernel, 99.83% (small training set) / 99.87% (large); JPEG quality 90, 86.06% (small) / 90.90% (large); resizing scale 1.01, 96.01% (small) / 95.11% (large). These numbers characterize a different task (whole-patch manipulation classification with a trained SVM) and should not be quoted as Splicebuster's own splicing-localization accuracy.

**Relevance to our training-free localizer on Korus:** Splicebuster's *stated* default mode (per-image blind EM, no prior information, no training set) is exactly the training-free profile we need, and if the underlying algorithmic details could be recovered it would be a strong complementary cue alongside the CFA and noise-variance methods above - a texture-of-residual descriptor is a genuinely different signal from either a CFA-lattice correlation or a plain noise-variance estimate, so INFERENCE suggests it could catch tampering the other two miss (e.g. same-camera copy-paste where noise variance and CFA phase both match but the exact denoising/sharpening history differs slightly). However, because neither the WIFS paper's own text nor its official code (MATLAB/Python zips, confirmed to have existed on the GRIP download server but not retrievable this session) could be obtained, **we do not currently have enough verified detail to reimplement Splicebuster's actual decision mechanism** - only its shared feature-extraction front end (via the arXiv proxy paper) and its high-level abstract claim. **Limitations the authors themselves state:** `not verified from retrieved text` for Splicebuster's own stated limitations - the WIFS paper's discussion/conclusion section was not reachable.
